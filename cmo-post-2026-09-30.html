I'm ICF-certified. That's not why clients hire me. I did the 100+ hours of classes. I did the 100+ hours of coaching real clients. I'm proud of the certificate and I'll tell you it exists, but I don't lead with it, because nobody has ever been pulled through a hard decision by a piece of paper. What I lead with is this. I was laid off after Lehman in 2009, and again at a London hedge fund in 2017. In between, I sold almost everything I owned at 30, jobless, to start my own fund, and got turned down 50 times before anyone seeded it. Then I bought preschools in Singapore, led a team of about 50, and sold the group. I share the above not to solicit sympathy or envy. Rather, I want you to see why it matters when you're the one sitting across from me, scared to leave the salary, wondering if you're the only one who has ever felt this stuck. A certified coach can ask you good questions. Someone who has lost the job, the business card and the income can ask the ones that hurt, and can tell when your answer is the polite one. So before you pay anyone to coach you, test them. Ask what they've lost. Ask what they've rebuilt. Ask what they'd do differently. DM me the word TESTED and I'll send you the 3 questions I wish someone had asked me before I paid for coaching. P.S. The certificate taught me how to ask. The two layoffs taught me when to stop asking and act.

Extend

The certificate did teach me one thing I still use: a good question beats a good answer. The 100+ coaching hours made me patient enough to wait for the real answer, the one that comes after the polite one. The layoffs are what let me recognise it when it arrives.

Humanise

The second layoff, in 2017, hurt more than the first. I had already been David once, and I knew exactly what the next few months would feel like. No certification class prepared me for that. The only thing that helped was the person who had been there before me.
