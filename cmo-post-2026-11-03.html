Post (Story-Flow, Variation B)
I looked into the mirror the week after my second layoff and the person looking back at me asked a question I did not want to answer. It was 2017, a London hedge fund, and I had been here before. In 2009 I told myself it was the crisis, the market, everyone's problem. This time there was no crisis to blame, and no business card left to hide behind, so the only thing in the room was me. The cost came first. The income stopped, the identity went with it, and I sat there wondering who I was when the title was gone and whether I had spent my best years being very good at the wrong thing. Then came the soul-searching, and it was not pretty. I had sold almost everything at 30 to start my own fund, collected 50 rejections before anyone seeded it, and still ended up exactly where I started, waiting on someone else to decide my next move. That was the moment I stopped asking "which firm will hire me?" and started asking "what would I regret on my death bed?" I share the above not to solicit any sympathy. Rather, the key takeaway is this: the layoff did not take my path away. It showed me I had only ever been following one that somebody else paved. A year later I was learning how to run a preschool in Singapore, a numbers guy the only man in a room of women I asked to teach me everything. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. P.S. If you've been through your own layoff, what did the mirror tell you? I read every reply.
