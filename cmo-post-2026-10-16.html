Being present is not the same as being seen. Half the meetings you attended this week didn't need you. I used to confuse being in the room with being useful. After years in banking, private equity and running my own hedge fund, a full calendar was my proof of worth, and I wore it like a medal. Then I moderated seven panels in one summit for C-suite and family office heads, and learned the job was never airtime. It was the next question. Later, leading about 50 preschool staff as a numbers guy in a room of educators, I learned it from the other side: my presence only mattered when I knew exactly why I was there. So before I show up to anything now, I ask three questions. 1. Does my presence change the outcome? If the decision happens with or without me, the room needs my notes, not my body. 2. What do I actually need to say? One thing, said once, beats ten comments made to be noticed. 3. What should I stay silent on? Someone closer to the work already has the answer, and my silence is how they get to give it. I share this not to shame a full calendar, rather, the key takeaway is that leadership is a set of questions, not face time. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. Save this and ask the three questions before your first meeting on Monday. P.S. On my death bed, I won't regret the meetings I skipped.
