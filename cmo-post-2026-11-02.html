I used to think a calendar with no white space proved I mattered. Back-to-back meetings, a stable and cushy job with decent pay, and I still wasn't fulfilled, because being present is not the same as being seen, and being busy is not the same as having impact. So here is the Meeting Audit I'd run on any executive's week. It takes one sitting. Open every recurring meeting on next week's calendar. Sort each one into a single bucket: 🫩 Keep: the decision changes because you are in the room. 🫩 Send a delegate: the information matters, your face doesn't. 🫩 Decline: you go out of habit, or because staying away feels risky. The Decline pile is where the truth sits. It is the business card at work. Your title gets you invited, so you keep showing up to prove the title was earned, and nobody in the room needs it proved. Quality over quantity is not a slogan. A leader in five meetings that move something is more present than one in twenty-five who nods along on mute. Give it a week and watch which meeting nobody notices you missed. Then spend those hours where the decision actually gets made. I share this not to preach, rather because I spent years confusing face time with impact, and no one told me the calendar was the habit to break first. P.S. Run the audit on next week's calendar before Sunday night, then tell me which meeting you're keeping out of habit.
