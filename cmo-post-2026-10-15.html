Who says the person coaching you should have never been fired, never failed, never bet on themselves? I hold a coaching certification. 100+ hours of classes, 100+ hours coaching real clients, and I'm proud of it. But here is what that certificate did not teach me. It did not teach me how it feels to be laid off after Lehman in 2009, and again at a London hedge fund in 2017, when the plan you built your identity on is simply gone. It did not teach me what 50 rejections sound like when you are 30, jobless, and have sold almost everything you own to seed your own fund. And it did not teach me how to lead a team of 50 through a business I had never run before, then hand it over at exit. A bad year taught me that. A certificate shows you finished a course. A bad year shows you what you do when the plan breaks. I share the above not to solicit sympathy or applause, rather, the key takeaway is this. When a client like David calls me the day he is made redundant, I am not reciting a framework. I am remembering what that felt like from the inside. Experience without certificates beats certificates without experience. Ideally you have both, but if you have to pick one, pick the person who has been through it. P.S. What did one bad year teach you that no course, degree, or certificate ever did?

Extend

To be clear, I am not against certification. I earned mine and I would do it again. The order of operations is what matters. The certificate tells you someone can run a session. The bad year tells you whether they can stay in the room with you when the script runs out. Before you hire any coach, ask them one question: what was your worst year, and what did you do in month three of it?

Humanise

At 30, jobless, with almost everything sold, I heard no from 50 people before one said yes to my fund. Every no stung. Every no also meant I was one closer to the yes. I would not trade that stretch for any course I have taken.
