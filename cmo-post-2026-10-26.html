The Buy-Don't-Quit Ladder

Pillar: Freedom Through Ownership (ETA) · Format: Framework post (Variation A) · Intent: Educating

Today's post
I told a client to stop planning his resignation, and it was the best advice he got. He had the runway math done and the speech for his boss half written, because every founder story he had read said you jump first and figure it out later. So he assumed freedom meant quitting. You don't need to quit. I started as David, laid off and panicking, because a salary was my only income. What trapped me was never the job. It was having one source of income. So here is the Buy-Don't-Quit Ladder, three rungs: 🫩 Rung 1: Keep the salary. It is your compounding engine, and it lets you buy without panic. 🫩 Rung 2: Buy a business that a hired operator runs. You own it. You don't become its employee. 🫩 Rung 3: Add the next one, funded by the first. Janet kept her corporate job and acquired accounting practices on the side. She can now replace her income and is weighing a full exit. Kelvin, an exit founder, bought laundromats and runs a portfolio netting $500K a year. I share the above not to solicit any sense of envy, but because "grind as a founder or stay trapped" is a false choice. I bought and scaled a preschool group to 50+ staff and 10x+ revenue before I exited, and none of it began with a resignation letter. I started as David, became Janet, then Kelvin. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did: I kept the salary and bought the first one. P.S. Send this to the friend who keeps saying next year.

Extend

On rung 2, the hired operator is the whole point. You own the cash flow and the decisions that matter, and someone else runs the day. That is how a salary buys freedom without you swapping one job for another.

Humanise

At 30 I sold almost everything I had, jobless, to start my hedge fund. I know what it costs to jump first. That is why I now tell people to keep the salary and let it fund the next move.
