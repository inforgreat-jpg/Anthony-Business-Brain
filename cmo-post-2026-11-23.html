Full post
I used to think the person who spoke most in the room was leading it. I believed that through my years at Credit Suisse and into private equity in Hong Kong, where at 27 I sat across from the heads of Chinese state-owned banks with nobody next to me, and I filled every silence because I was convinced that if I wasn't talking I wasn't earning my seat, and it took me two layoffs to notice that the people who moved the room were rarely the ones who spoke first. I wasn't more present. I was louder. Now I ask three questions before I walk into any meeting. 1. When does my presence actually matter here? Rarely the whole hour. Usually one or two moments: the decision, or the second a senior person hesitates. 2. What will I say? One sentence I'd be fine hearing quoted back to me. If I can't write it before I walk in, I'm not ready to speak. 3. What will I stay silent for? Often the first ten minutes. Let everyone else show you where the real disagreement sits. I share this not to sound wise, but because I spent years confusing face time with impact, and my calendar looked full while my days felt empty. You don't need another plan. You need to take one action today. Pick one meeting tomorrow. Stay silent for the first ten minutes. Save this post so you remember, then tell me what you heard that you would have talked over. P.S. Silence isn't absence. It's where the room tells you what it needs.

Pinned comments

Extend

The three questions work because they shrink the meeting. Question 1 is the one people skip. Most of an hour is not your moment. Find the one or two minutes that are, and prepare only for those.

Humanise

What I never said in the post: at 27 I thought silence would expose me as the youngest person at the table. It did the opposite. The quiet is where I learned what the other side was afraid to ask for.

CTA Amplifier

Try it tomorrow. One meeting, ten silent minutes. Come back and tell me what you heard that you would have talked over. If you know someone who always has the last word, send them this.
