Most coaches were trained. They were never tested. I am ICF-certified. I sat through the hours, logged the sessions, passed what needed passing. The certificate was the easy half. Before you hand someone $6,000 to coach you through a pivot, a promotion, or an exit, ask them these five questions. I wish someone had asked me these before I hired my first coach. One. Have you personally lost the job, the salary, the identity you're coaching me to protect? I was laid off twice, once after Lehman in 2009 and once from a hedge fund I started myself. Reading about job loss and living it are not the same pain. Two. Have you built something and watched part of it fail anyway? I grew a preschool group in Singapore past 50 staff and still lost one school to a culture I could never fix, no matter who I replaced. Three. Can you tell me exactly what you would have done differently, not vaguely? Vague answers usually mean vague experience. Four. Do you have a process, or do you have scars plus a process? A process alone is theory. Scars alone is chaos. Both together is a map built from the road. Five. What's the worst advice you were ever paid to give, and did you admit it was wrong? A coach who has never been wrong in public hasn't been tested in public either. I ask myself all five before I charge anyone. Save this before your next coaching call. Which of these have you never asked? P.S. The certificate got me in the room. The scars are why clients stay.
