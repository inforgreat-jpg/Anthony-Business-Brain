Full Post

There are three types of people who come to me after losing their job. Only one of them ends up financially free.
I call them David, Janet, and Kelvin. Every career changer I meet fits one of these three stages. David just got made redundant, second time in his career. He is panicking, applying to jobs at midnight, quietly terrified that his one income source is now zero. Janet has been through that fire already. She kept a corporate job but started buying small side businesses on weekends. Safety net in one hand, ownership in the other. Kelvin sold his last acquisition and built a $500K a year portfolio. No boss. No business card. Just a stack of decisions he made when nobody was watching. Here is what nobody tells David at midnight. David, Janet, and Kelvin are the same person, just at different points of the same decision. The identity was never the job title. Your business card gets taken away the day you least expect it, and if that card was your entire sense of self, you lose more than an income. You lose your direction. The nos David gets from job applications are not proof he is finished. They are proof he is closer to the yes that actually fits him. I share this not to make redundancy sound easy. It is not. David becomes Kelvin the moment he decides his next move will be chosen, not handed to him. So here is what I will ask you. Right now, this week, which one are you? David, Janet, or Kelvin?
