David panicked. Janet bought. Kelvin exited. Same economy, three different responses, and I have been all three. David was just made redundant. His salary was his only income, so every week without an offer felt like a countdown. I know that feeling because I was laid off in 2009 and again in 2017, and both times I treated a job as the only door I had. Janet kept her corporate job and acquired accounting practices on the side. She did not quit and she did not stay trapped. She let the practices grow until the income could replace her salary, and only then did she start thinking about a full exit. Kelvin had already built and exited a company. He bought laundromats, and that portfolio now nets him about $500K a year. I share the above not to solicit any sense of envy or fear. Rather, the key takeaway is that the choice was never quit now and grind, or stay and feel stuck. There is a third path, and it starts with the salary you already earn. I started as David, became Janet, then Kelvin. My version of the laundromats was a preschool group in Singapore, scaled over six years and sold in a strategic exit. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. I stopped asking which job to take next and started asking what I could own. P.S. DM me the word OWN and I'll send you the ETA Masterclass details.
