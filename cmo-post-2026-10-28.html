I was good at everything I was told to be good at. So why did it feel off? Credit Suisse M&A. Private equity in Hong Kong. Negotiating with bank heads on my own at 27. On paper, I had made it, and then I was laid off after Lehman in 2009, and I did what every high performer does, which is try to figure it out alone, so I went around in circles with the same head that had built the problem in the first place. Then I sold almost everything I had at 30 and started my own hedge fund. Fifty people said no before anyone seeded it. Then a London fund laid me off in 2017. Second time. I looked into the mirror and the person looking back at me asked the same question again. This time I did not try to answer it alone. I share this not to solicit sympathy. Rather, the key takeaway is this: every time I got unstuck, it was because I stopped inventing the route and followed one somebody had already paved. Like fledglings watching the first one jump off the cliff. Being stuck is not a gap in your talent. It is a gap in who has walked it before you. That's what I do with senior professionals now, 1:1. I do not hand you a playbook, because I never had one either. I ask better questions, tell you what I got wrong, and we build the plan you actually choose. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. P.S. If this is you, DM me the word PIVOT.
