Everyone tells you the choice is quit and grind, or stay and stay stuck. Kelvin did neither. I know both corners well, because I started as David, laid off and panicking with the one income I had suddenly gone, and for a long time I believed those were the only two doors in the room, so I either had to bet everything on a new venture or hope the next employer would never do the same thing to me again. Kelvin kept his salary. He bought laundromats and hired operators to run them, and he let the cash flow compound while his paycheck covered life. Today that portfolio nets about $500K a year, and he got there faster than he would have by quitting, because he never had to restart from zero every time a month went badly. I share this not to solicit envy. The point is that the salary was never the cage. It was the engine. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. I became Janet, then Kelvin, and my own version of it was preschools in Singapore, a team of 50-plus, and an exit in 2024. You do not need the courage to resign. You need a plan to buy something that pays you while you keep earning. That is what I teach inside the ETA Masterclass, for senior professionals who want ownership without the leap. P.S.
