I ran the actual numbers on staying in my last job for one more year. Not the salary. The real cost. It came out close to six figures once I counted everything I would have built instead. Most people never do that math. They just renew the excuse. "One more year until it's safer." I said the same thing at 29, sitting in a hedge fund seat that looked good from the outside and felt like a stalemate from the inside. Here is the three step calculation I now walk every client through before they renew that excuse again. One. Name the actual trade off. Not "I'm staying for stability." Write down what the year costs: the equity you're not building, the business you're not starting, the acquisition someone else is closing while you wait. Two. Price the alternative. What could that same year compound into if you put it toward the business instead of the job? Not a guess. A real number. Three. Decide with the number in front of you, not the fear behind you. Fear does not do math. It just repeats itself in different words every January. I left that seat at 30 and took a real pay cut to build something of my own. That one decision made me financially free years before most of my peers, because I ran the number instead of running the excuse. Your calendar already shows you what you're choosing every single week. Most people just never read it that way. Save this and run your own three steps this weekend. Then look at what your calendar actually says about what you value.
