I signed the deal knowing nothing about preschools. At 40, after a career built entirely in banking, private equity, and running my own hedge fund, I bought my first one in Singapore. Not for some grand plan. I just couldn't find a preschool I liked for my own kid. Most people think acquiring a business profitably starts with the deal. The multiple. The structure. That part was the easy part. Low single digit EBITDA multiples. Straightforward math. What almost broke me was the team. I walked into an organization that was 100% women, and I was the only man in the room. So I told them straight: you're my mentors, teach me everything. The curriculum stayed the same at first, then improved over time. The philosophy changed almost immediately. Let kids fall. Let them get a worksheet wrong before you correct it. Teach them about money starting at age four. One of the five schools I bought had a culture I could not fix, no matter how many staff I replaced. That's the one I lost. You can buy a client base and systems. You cannot buy culture. It's already there when you sign, good or broken. The ones that worked, I digitized what was still running on USB sticks and raised fees where the value already justified it. Revenue across the group grew more than 10x. When I sold, I found the buyer through my own network. No broker. A profitable exit. Who says a numbers guy can't run a preschool? Fall forward. P.S.
