The day I signed the papers for the preschool group, I didn't feel like an owner. I felt like a fraud. I'd spent my whole career in banking, private equity, then my own hedge fund. Numbers, structures, term sheets. None of that prepared me for walking into a room of teachers and realizing I was the only man in it, and the only one who had never taught a child anything in his life. For the first ninety days I second guessed every decision. I kept the curriculum exactly as it was because I didn't trust myself to know what should change. I told the team straight: you're my mentors now, teach me everything. Some nights I lay awake wondering if I'd just spent everything I had on a mistake I didn't understand yet. Then the numbers started to move. Not because I'd found some clever multiple or negotiated harder. Because I'd finally stopped pretending to know and started actually listening. We digitized what was still running on paper and USB sticks. We raised fees where the value already justified it. Revenue across the group grew more than 10x before I sold it. I didn't feel like a fraud the day I signed. I felt like one for the ninety days after. The ownership showed up quietly, somewhere in month four, when I stopped asking permission to have an opinion in my own company. Who says a numbers guy can't run a preschool. If you're sitting on a business card that isn't really yours yet, tell me where you are in that story.
