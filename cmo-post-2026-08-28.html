I used to think the panic after a layoff meant something had gone wrong. It just means you are on stage one of three, and most people never learn the other two. Every client who comes to me after a layoff or mid-pivot starts here. Where they end up depends on whether they ever learn which stage they are in. Stage one is panic. David had just been made redundant and his only income, his salary, was gone. He told me he didn't know what he was worth without the title. We didn't start with a job search. We started with what nobody could repossess: his skills, his judgement, his relationships. Stage two is compounding. Janet stayed in her corporate accounting job and acquired small accounting practices on the side, treating it as something to get serious about eventually. The shift wasn't a leap. It was the day she ran the side practice like the real business, until it could replace her salary. Stage three is portfolio. Kelvin exited his last company and bought laundromats. Not one. A portfolio that clears $500K a year without him standing behind a single counter. Redundancy was never the crisis for any of them. Believing the salary was the only thing they owned was. Know which stage you're in? Save this. Not sure? DM me the word STAGE and I'll tell you what the next one actually requires. P.S. I started as David. I've been Janet. I'm closer to Kelvin now than I've ever been, and none of it happened in the order a resume would suggest.
