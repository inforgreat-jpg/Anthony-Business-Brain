Hook · Result Lead (chosen)A VP I coach reclaimed twelve hours a week from her calendar without dropping a single responsibility.
She didn't quit her job. She didn't hire an assistant. She asked one question before saying yes to any invite: does my presence actually change this outcome? Most executives think a full calendar is proof they matter. I used to think that too, back when I was running a hedge fund and treated every meeting invite like an obligation I couldn't say no to. It took me years to realize being in the room is not the same as being needed in it. Here is the audit she now runs before every meeting on her calendar. Three questions, asked in order. 1. Does my presence change the decision, or just witness it? If the outcome is the same whether you show up or not, you are not leading. You are attending. 2. Is there someone on my team who should be making this call instead of me? Every meeting you sit in that someone else could run is a meeting you are quietly telling that person they are not ready for. 3. What actually breaks if I am not in this room? Not what feels uncomfortable. What actually breaks. Most of the time, the honest answer is nothing. She ran this against every meeting on her calendar for two weeks. Half of them failed the test. Presence is not proof of leadership. Knowing when your presence changes the outcome, that is the whole job. Save this before your next calendar invite lands. P.S.
