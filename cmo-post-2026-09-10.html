A certificate doesn't mean someone's ever been tested. Being laid off twice does. I am ICF-certified. I have the same credential most executive coaches lean on to prove they know what they're doing. It's not what makes me useful to a client staring down a pivot. What makes me useful is that I've been laid off twice, once after Lehman in 2009 and again from a London hedge fund in 2017, and both times had to answer the question every framework skips: what happens to your identity when the job disappears and the certificate is still sitting in a drawer somewhere. At 30, jobless, I sold almost everything I owned to seed a hedge fund in the middle of a financial crisis. Fifty people told me no before one said yes. Nobody's coaching certification prepares them for fifty nos. A framework can tell a client what smart people generally do next. It cannot tell them what it actually costs to be wrong, because the person teaching it has never had to find out. That's the gap. Not credentials versus no credentials. Tested versus untested. Anyone can choose who they learn from. I'd rather learn from someone who has already paid the price of being wrong, certificate or not. What's one piece of advice you got from someone who's never actually lived it? P.S. The certificate isn't the problem. Pretending it's the same as the scar is.
