# VOICE DNA PROFILE
## Anthony Tse

## SECTION 1: CONVERSATIONAL MARKERS

**Primary fillers:** "so," "actually," "basically," and "yes" used as a mid-sentence affirmation rather than a direct answer to a question.

- "So every day I would wake up with excitement." (Q8)
- "I basically said, 'Yes I'm happy to have a divorce if certain things are reached.'" (Q7)
- "My client feels that this message that's being passed on is, yes, he's actually being a good messenger." (Q11)

Frequency: heavy user. "So" opens sentences repeatedly across separate answers (Q5, Q8), and "yes" appears as a stand-alone affirmation-filler three separate times (Q6, Q7, Q11) — not answering a question, just punctuating a claim.

**Transition phrases:** "More importantly" and "Ultimately" carry almost all of his pivots from story to lesson.

- "More importantly the fact that I'm very open about my mistakes makes me unique..." (Q10)
- "Ultimately what I value is impact, whether or not I'm making someone's life better..." (Q12)
- "...but ultimately what that taught me was the ability to move on independently..." (Q6)

**Sentence openers:** Three dominant patterns — "So..." (Q5, Q8), a blunt one-line fact ("Time is limited." — Q3), or a rhetorical question fragment ("Don't have a system? They don't plan?" — Q9).

## SECTION 2: SENTENCE PATTERNS

**Average length:** Highly variable by design — long unbroken run-ons (30–60+ words) sit right next to very short declaratives (3–6 words).

**Preferred structure:**
- Short punchy declarations: "Time is limited." (Q3) / "I don't use theory." (Q10) / "I absolutely hate using theory or research." (Q10)
- Long flowing run-ons with minimal internal punctuation, clauses chained by "and," "so," "but yet": the entirety of Q1 is one continuous sentence — "Escaped a car accident at the age of 16 where I saw my friend die at the scene but I was fortunate enough to have escaped the same car accident made me realize that life is fleeting..."
- Rhetorical fragments used to open an answer: "Don't have a system? They don't plan?" (Q9)

**Sentence variety:** Deliberate swing between clipped declaratives and long unbroken run-ons, often inside the same answer — Q10 opens with the four-word idiom "Anybody who's been there, done that," then immediately runs into a much longer sentence.

**Opening patterns:** (1) "So..." (2) a direct declarative statement (3) a rhetorical question fragment.

## SECTION 4: EMOTIONAL SIGNATURE

**Primary tone:** Reflective and conviction-driven — direct and confident without being boastful. Failure and hardship are consistently reframed as the source of his value, not despite the cost but because of it.

- "It's the fact that I failed and made mistakes that makes me valuable." (Q10)
- "What this taught me is that life is not always a straight line and that taking detours actually could be healthy and could lead you to something better." (Q6)

**Secondary tone (crisis/breakthrough moments):** Matter-of-fact and almost procedural. The near head-on collision in Q5 is narrated with mechanical, blow-by-blow detail ("I swerved back into my lane just as I passed the car in front of me...") rather than dramatic language — composure under pressure reads as part of his identity, not an afterthought.

**Tonal shifts:** Noticeably warmer and more emotionally open discussing purpose, family, and impact (Q3, Q7, Q8, Q13, Q15); flatter and more procedural narrating crisis events (Q4, Q5).

**Confidence markers:**
- Certain: flat absolutes — "I absolutely hate using theory or research." (Q10) / "This is not the right job for them." (Q11)
- Reflective/uncertain: turns a value into a question aimed at himself — "Is how I spend my time, energy, and resources aligned with my values and purpose?" (Q3)

## SECTION 6: IDEA FLOW PATTERN

**Dominant pattern:** Concrete moment/fact → specific cost → specific gain → "what that taught me." E.g., Q6: gave up income at 30 → shareholder dispute forced a lawsuit → "financially free, mentally free, before most people" → "life is not always a straight line."

**How he builds credibility:** Through openly disclosed personal failure, backed by quoted reactions from other people rather than by claims or credentials — he quotes himself ("Yes I'm happy to have a divorce..." Q7), quotes admirers ("Wow how come you're able to do this?" Q12), and quotes clients ("Hey I remember this from Anthony's coaching session..." Q15).

**How he handles complexity:** Reduces it to one plain analogy (darts thrown in the dark, steering a ship) or one personal anecdote rather than an abstract explanation.

**How he closes a thought:** Lands on a flat value statement or aphorism-like conclusion ("that's how I define success" — Q12; "Typical example of turning something negative and daunting into positive" — Q11) rather than a question or a call to action.

## SECTION 7: AUTHENTICITY MARKERS

**Communication quirks:**
- Writes in unbroken, dictation-style run-on sentences with minimal internal punctuation — reads like transcribed speech, not edited prose (the entirety of Q1 is one sentence).
- Drops casual mid-thought labels into otherwise personal storytelling — "Process:" (Q13), "Say my marriage:" (Q7) — a business-systems habit leaking into personal narrative.
- Proves a point through quoted dialogue rather than assertion, in every single story that has another person in it (Q7, Q12, Q15).
- Uses a concessive "Yes... but/while" structure to name the cost before claiming the win — "Yes while it may have cost me financially... I became financially free, mentally free." (Q6)
- Opens answers with either one blunt declarative fact ("Time is limited." — Q3) or a rhetorical fragment ("Don't have a system? They don't plan?" — Q9).
- Pairs two matching adjectives against the same noun for emphasis — "financially free, mentally free" (Q6).

**Perspective patterns:**
- Reframes negative events as the direct source of value, not despite the failure but because of it (Q10).
- Treats control/agency as the emotional core of hard periods — frustration is specifically about lack of control; resolution comes specifically from reclaiming it (Q4).
- Measures success by alignment (values/purpose) and impact, explicitly naming and discarding the old metrics — title, salary, air miles, hotel perks (Q12).

**Personal touches:** Names his father directly as a formative influence (Q2); discusses his marriage and a near-divorce ultimatum candidly and without euphemism (Q7); narrates his own physical fear in a near-death moment with procedural, first-person detail (Q5).

**Values that come through:** Autonomy/control (Q4, Q6, Q7) · honesty about failure (Q10) · impact over income (Q8, Q13, Q15) · presence and no regrets (Q1, Q3, Q12).

## SECTION 8: RED FLAGS — WHAT HE'D NEVER SAY

**Language he avoids:** No corporate jargon anywhere across 15 answers — no "synergy," "leverage" (in the business-speak sense), "circle back," "value-add." No hedging softeners like "I feel like maybe" — even reflective statements are flat and declarative.

**Specific violations:**

✗ NEVER write: "I leveraged my network to pivot my career trajectory."
Why: He never uses "leverage" or "trajectory" — his verbs are concrete and often physical (steer, extricate, swerve, craft), not corporate abstractions.

✗ NEVER write: "It was a challenging but ultimately rewarding journey of growth."
Why: Too vague. His actual pattern names the specific cost first (financially, mentally, in years given up) before naming the specific gain — never a blanket "growth" claim with no numbers or details attached.

✗ NEVER write clean, fully punctuated, short-paragraph prose with no run-ons.
Why: His authentic rhythm is dictation-style — long sentences chained by "and," "so," "but yet" are how he actually thinks out loud. Over-editing into tidy short paragraphs flattens the voice.

✗ NEVER tell a story without one concrete, filmable detail.
Why: Every hard story he tells has a specific physical or verbal moment attached — the swerve on the German highway, the exact line he said to his wife, six years of running the fund. Abstraction isn't how he tells stories.

✗ NEVER end a story on a purely negative or unresolved note.
Why: Every answer resolves toward a lesson, a reframe, or a named gain — even the near head-on collision and the marriage ultimatum land on resolution, not lingering dread.

**Structural violations:**
- Never opens with a thesis statement. He opens with the moment or the fact; the "point" of the story arrives at the end, not the beginning.
- Never lists credentials as proof of credibility. Proof always arrives through a quoted reaction from someone else, not a claim about himself.
