Post (hook highlighted)
I used to wear a full calendar like a medal. Back-to-back calls, a title on my business card, and a quiet belief that if I was busy enough, I must be doing something that mattered. Then I got laid off. Twice. And what I noticed both times was how little of what had filled those weeks I actually missed. A full calendar is not proof of a full life. Half of what's booked on yours today, you could delete and nothing real would change. No client lost. No deal dead. Just a meeting that exists because someone needed to feel needed, and you said yes because yes was easier than the conversation about why not. That's linear conditioning. Put in the hours, collect the promotion, never ask who the hours were for. I share this not to judge anyone's schedule, rather, the key takeaway is the filter I started using: lying on my death bed, would I regret how I was spending this hour? Most of my recurring meetings failed it in seconds. What passed were the things I'd have to fight for. Building the business. Watching my first child graduate from a preschool I owned. Anyone can choose what stays on the calendar. Here's what I did. I deleted the ones that failed first, and I stopped apologizing for the empty space. P.S. What's one recurring thing on your calendar you'd delete tomorrow if you were honest?
