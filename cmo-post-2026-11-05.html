Full post
Who says the person you learn from should never have been told no? I was 30, jobless, and had sold almost everything I owned to start a hedge fund in the middle of the financial crisis. I came with Credit Suisse and private equity behind me, plenty of conviction, and a pitch I could recite in my sleep. What I did not have was a yes. It took 50 nos before someone finally seeded the fund. Fifty. And every one of them peeled a little more of the business card off me, until what was left was the part that actually had to do the work. That fund went on to be nominated Best New Asian Hedge Fund in 2014. I share the above not to solicit applause. The key takeaway is that nobody can teach you how to hear no 50 times from a slide deck, and I have yet to meet a certificate that has been told no once, let alone 50 times. I hold an ICF certification myself, so I am not knocking the paper. The paper is where credibility starts. The scars are the rest of it, and they show up in the room within about ten minutes. When you pick someone to learn from, ask what the biggest no they ever collected was, and what they did the next morning. Then listen to how long the pause is. Anyone can choose to keep asking. Here's what I did. P.S. How many nos have you collected this year, and which one taught you the most?
