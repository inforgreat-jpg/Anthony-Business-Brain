Full post
David called me the day he was made redundant. He wasn't sad. He was scared. Those are not the same thing. Sad is about what you lost. Scared is about the paycheck that isn't coming, because his salary was the only income he had and the whole life he'd built only worked as long as one company kept saying yes. I knew that call. I've been laid off twice, after Lehman in 2009 and from a London hedge fund in 2017, and both times my first few days sounded exactly like David, every plan I made was a plan to get another business card as fast as possible because without one I didn't know what to call myself. Here's what I couldn't see then. Panic is a poor advisor. It only knows how to rebuild what you just lost, so it sends you straight back to the same job and the same single paycheck. What changed things was one question. If the card is gone, what do I still have? Skills that travel, a few people who still picked up my calls, and a clear sense of what I'd regret lying on my death bed. That list became my first real option, and it wasn't a job. It was a hedge fund, and 50 people said no before anyone seeded it. I share the above not to solicit sympathy. Rather, the key takeaway is that a layoff is the one moment you finally get to choose, if you let the panic pass first. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. P.S. If you've been through a layoff, or you're quietly bracing for one, tell me what the first week was like for you.

Extend

The list I made had three columns: skills that travel, people who still picked up, and what I'd regret on my death bed. Nothing on it had a company name. That was the point. When the card is gone, only the first three things are left, and they are enough to start from.

Humanise

Honestly, what scared me most wasn't the money. It was the first dinner where someone asked what I do and I didn't have a card to answer for me. If that's where you are right now, it passes, and it's more common than anyone admits.

CTA Amplifier

Try it tonight: write down what you'd still have if your business card disappeared tomorrow. If the list is shorter than you expected, that's worth a conversation. DM me and tell me where you are, and I'll tell you what I'd look at first.
