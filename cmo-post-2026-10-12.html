The post
You can tell me what you earn per year. Can you tell me what one hour of your time is actually worth? I couldn't, back when my finance job was stable, cushy and decent pay, and I still looked into the mirror wondering who was looking back at me. So I built a test. Three steps. I run it on myself and I walk my coaching clients through it. 1. Price the hour you sell. Take your total pay and divide it by the hours you really work, not the 40 on your contract. Add the 10pm emails and the Sunday dread. Most senior people never do this math because the answer is uncomfortable. 2. Price the hour you could own. Same hour, inside a business you own. When I scaled a preschool group to about 50 staff and revenue grew more than 10x, my hours stopped being a salary and started compounding. That gap is the whole point of this step. 3. Audit where the hour actually goes. Open your calendar. Next to every block, write what it paid you: money, learning or joy. Anything that paid you nothing was not an accident, it was a decision you never made on purpose. I share this not to make you quit tomorrow. Rather, the key takeaway is that most of us negotiate our salary harder than we guard our time, and a business card only ever shows the title, never what an hour costs. Anyone can choose to price their hour. Here's what I did: I stopped selling mine cheaply. P.S. Save this and run the test on your own calendar tonight.
