Post · Variation A (Framework-Heavy)

You can tell me what you earn per year. Can you tell me what one hour of your time is actually worth? Most senior people I meet can't. I couldn't either, until I was laid off twice and realised my employer had been pricing my hours the whole time, and I had never once asked what number they used. Here is the Hourly-Worth Test. Three steps, one evening, one calculator. 1. Price the hour you sell. Take your annual pay and divide it by the hours you really work, not the hours on your contract. That is what one buyer pays for your hour. It is not what your hour is worth. 2. Price the same hour inside something you own. Kelvin kept his salary and bought laundromats run by hired operators, so his hours stopped being the product. When I scaled my preschool group, revenue grew more than 10x through a team of about 50, not through my own hours. 3. Audit where the hour actually goes. Open last week's calendar. Mark every block as sold, built or lost. Be honest about the third column, the hours that left your calendar without buying you anything. I share this not to push you to quit. The key takeaway is that a salary is one price for your time, and most of us accepted it without ever comparing. Save this and run the test on your own calendar tonight. P.S. Lying on my death bed, I would regret the hours I never priced.

Extend

If step 1 gave you a number, run it again with one change: divide by every hour work touches, including the commute, the Sunday night inbox and the dread. Most people find their hour is worth less than they thought, which is exactly why step 2 matters.

Humanise

Step 3 is the one people skip, and I understand why. Back when I was a salaried banker, I did not want to see that column either. If your lost column is the longest, you are not behind. You are finally looking.

CTA Amplifier

If you know someone who is quietly good at their job and quietly tired of it, send them this post. Repost it to your network, and tell me which column was longest for you: sold, built or lost.
