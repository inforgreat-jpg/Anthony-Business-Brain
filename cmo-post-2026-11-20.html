I have been living on borrowed time since I was six, when a surgeon opened my chest to repair the heart I was born with, and it is the reason I count something most of my peers never think to count. Weekends. If you are 45 and you get to 80, you have about 1,800 weekends left. Not years. Weekends. Most senior executives I coach have never done that sum, so they keep handing those weekends to a calendar somebody else built, telling themselves they will slow down after the next promotion, the next deal, the next quarter. I call this the Borrowed Time Budget. It takes 15 minutes. 1. Count. Subtract your age from 80 and multiply by 52. Write that number at the top of the page. 2. List. Write down every hour of your coming week: meetings, commutes, scrolling, dinners you attend but are not present for. 3. Sort. Put each hour in one column: Keep, Shrink or Hand off. 4. Test. Keep what you would still choose lying on your death bed. Shrink what you would defend but not enjoy. Hand off what only you believe you can do. I share this not to solicit any sympathy. I was laid off twice, in 2009 and 2017, and no title I held ever gave a single weekend back. The number changes what you say yes to. Anyone can choose. The hours are yours until the day they are not. When was the last time you checked whether the hours you are spending are actually yours? P.S. Save this. Run the budget before Sunday night.

Extend

Worked example. You are 52 and plan to reach 80: 28 years times 52 is 1,456 weekends. Take out the ones you already know are spoken for by travel and obligations, and the number you actually control is smaller. That is the number the Keep column has to fit inside.

Humanise

One weekend I would never put in the Hand off column: watching my first child graduate from a preschool I had bought and built. Nobody sends a calendar invite for that one. You have to choose it ahead of time.
