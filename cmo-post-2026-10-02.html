You've been successful for years. And yet, something still doesn't feel right. That's not a sign you're broken. It's a sign you're following someone else's map. I was merely following a path somebody had paved for me: Penn, Credit Suisse M&A, private equity in Hong Kong, the next rung and then the next one, and on paper it looked like a straight line until the layoff after Lehman in 2009 and a second one in London in 2017 made me admit the line was a story I only told afterwards. Here is what my real map looked like. Four turns, none of them planned: 1. Laid off in 2009. The ladder was borrowed. A title is rented. 2. Hedge fund at 30. 50 rejections before anyone seeded it. Every no gets you closer to the one yes. 3. Laid off in London, 2017. It hurt less the second time. Fall forward. 4. Preschools in Singapore, 2018 to 2024. A numbers guy leading a team of about 50. Your skills travel further than your job title says. The enemy here is linear conditioning: the belief that there is only one map for a successful career. There never was. There is only the zig-zag and the hidden treasures you find on it. I share the above not to solicit sympathy. The key takeaway is that none of it was on the plan, and all of it is why I can sit across from you today and say I've been exactly where you are. P.S. Save this if your career has never been a straight line and you're tired of pretending it should be.

1. Extend

Looking back, the 50 rejections before the hedge fund were the best training I had for the second layoff. By 2017 I knew how to hear a no and keep moving. Every turn on the map trained me for the next one.

2. Humanise

None of this felt like a map while I was in it. In the middle of each turn I felt lost, and I only drew the line afterwards. If you feel lost in a turn right now, that doesn't mean you're behind.
