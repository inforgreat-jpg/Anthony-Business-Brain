Most coaches learned about risk in a classroom. I learned it the year I sold almost everything I owned and was jobless at 30. I am ICF-certified. A hundred hours of coursework, a hundred hours of client hours. So when someone tells me they already tried coaching and it didn't work, I don't defend the certification. I ask what actually happened in the room. Here's what nobody puts on a certificate. In 2008, mid financial crisis, I sold almost everything I owned and started a hedge fund with no job and 50 rejections behind me before anyone said yes. Nine years later, I got laid off again, this time from a hedge fund in London. Both times I had to decide who I was without the title on my business card. A framework can teach you the theory of a hard decision. It cannot teach you what it feels like to hold the outcome, because the outcome is yours, not a case study's. I later bought a group of preschools in Singapore and walked into a team of 50 women as the only man in the room. None of what came after that came from a certificate. It came from being wrong, scared, and still moving. So when a stuck executive asks why this is different from the coach they already tried, my answer is simple. Certification tells you what leadership should look like. Lived experience tells you what it costs. That's the gap the 1:1 Coaching Program is built to close. When was the last time you checked whether your coach has actually paid the price they're teaching you to pay?
