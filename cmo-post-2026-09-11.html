Lying on my death bed, I would not regret the meetings I skipped. I'd regret the years I spent performing a job instead of building a life. I did the math on that a few years ago. 168 hours in a week. I wrote down where every one of them actually went, not where I assumed they went. Here's the audit I now walk clients through before they touch a pivot, an acquisition, or even a Tuesday evening. 1. Count the real number. 168 hours. Not "I'm so busy." The actual number, same for everyone, from the CEO to the new hire. 2. Subtract the fixed costs. Sleep, work, commute. For most senior people that's 100 to 110 hours gone before you've made a single choice. 3. Look at what's left. 50 to 68 hours a week that you call "free time" and mostly hand to your phone, your inbox after hours, or obligations you never actually agreed to. 4. Ask the only question that matters. Of those hours, how many go toward the thing you'd actually want to have been building when this job ends, whether that's a business, a skill, or a relationship? When I ran this for myself while I was still collecting a salary and quietly building toward my hedge fund, the number was ugly. Four hours. Out of 168. I didn't need more time. I needed to stop lying to myself about where mine was already going. Save this if you've been meaning to actually look at where your 168 hours go this week.
