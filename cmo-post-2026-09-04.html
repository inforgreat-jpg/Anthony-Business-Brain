I told myself for two years I didn't have time to look at buying the preschool group. Then I tracked my calendar for seven days and found out that was a lie. 168 hours in a week. Same as everyone else, no matter what is printed on your business card. I logged mine hour by hour. Sleep, work, family, scrolling. All of it. Then I split the week into three columns. Column one: hours spent maintaining a life I already had. Meetings that existed because they always had. Errands that felt urgent and weren't. Column two: hours spent worrying about the pivot without moving it forward. Reading about acquisitions. Talking about the exit. Never touching a deal. Column three: hours spent building toward the decision. Calls with sellers. Numbers I ran myself. Conversations I was scared to have. Six hours. That's what landed in column three, out of 168. Values and purpose are supposed to be your GPS. You can have the nicest car and drive at top speed. But when most of your week sits in column one, you are not lost because you lack hours. You are lost because none of them point anywhere. Run the same audit before you tell yourself you don't have time for the pivot or the acquisition you keep circling. Track a week. Split it into the three columns. Look honestly at which one is winning. Save this if you've been meaning to audit your week. The number will tell you more than the excuse did. P.S. Six hours a week rebuilt the next decade. They had to be the right six.
