I have a client who freed up 12 hours a week by redefining one word. Presence. For years he believed it meant being in the room: every meeting, every review, every call where someone might notice he wasn't there, and so his calendar filled up and his evenings filled up, and the busier he got, the less it felt like leading. I asked him one question. When you sit in that meeting, what changes because you are there? For most of them, he had no answer. So he stopped going to the ones where he was only there to be seen. Twelve hours a week came back. Here is what surprised him. His team felt more trusted, not less. Nobody was waiting for him to watch the work. They needed him clear on where it was going and reachable when it counted. Most senior people I coach make the same trade without noticing. They swap showing up for showing impact, and talking for communicating, because the first is visible and the second is not. I share this not to say meetings are a waste. Rather, face time and impact are two different things, and nobody taught most of us the difference. I was merely following a path somebody had paved for me too, so I know how long you can do it without noticing. Anyone can choose what presence means on their own calendar. He did. P.S. What meeting did you attend this week purely to be seen?
