Full post
I looked into the mirror and the person looking back at me told me I wasn't fulfilled. That wasn't the first time. It kept happening. This was the hedge fund years. I had sold almost everything I had at 30, jobless, in the middle of a financial crisis, and been turned down 50 times before anyone seeded me. Then we were nominated for Best New Asian Hedge Fund in 2014. On paper, I was winning. And yet I'd stand there ready to play the part and feel hollow. Not tired. Hollow. I was showing up to every meeting and performing presence for people whose approval I had spent years chasing, and somewhere along the way it stopped being about the work and started being about how I looked doing it. I was scared to say it out loud, because who complains after 50 rejections and a nomination? So I stayed quiet, kept performing, and the mirror kept asking. Then one question landed. If the business card was taken away tomorrow, who would still be standing there? I didn't have a good answer. That's when I knew. I share the above not to solicit sympathy, envy or fear. Rather, the key takeaway is this: performing presence and being present look identical from the outside. Only you know which one you're doing. Anyone can choose. Here's what I did. I started running every decision through one filter: lying on my death bed, what would I regret? It was uncomfortable, and the first honest answer I'd had in years. P.S. If you've had your own mirror moment recently, tell me what it said to you.
