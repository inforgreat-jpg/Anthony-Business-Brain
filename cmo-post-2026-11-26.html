Getting laid off twice was the best career advice I never asked for. I was laid off after Lehman in 2009. Then again in 2017, from a London hedge fund. Both times I sat there asking the same question: what did I do wrong? Because I had been told a career is a ladder, and if you fall off a rung, you are behind. Nobody told me the ladder was someone else's idea. I went to Penn, landed at Credit Suisse M&A, and my first month included 9/11. I did everything the path asked of me. I was merely following a path that somebody had paved for me, like a fledgling watching the others jump off the cliff and following without ever asking if it was my cliff. Then 2009 stopped the path. At 30, jobless, I sold almost everything I had and started my own hedge fund. 50 rejections before anyone seeded it. Nominated Best New Asian Hedge Fund in 2014. I share this not to solicit sympathy, envy or fear. Here is what two layoffs actually handed me: ❤️ Proof that my identity was never my business card ❤️ The habit of asking, because if you don't ask, the answer is already a no ❤️ Skills that moved with me into preschools, boards and coaching Your career is not a ladder. It is a story. Anyone can choose. I just chose late. When was the last time you checked whether the path you're on is actually yours?
