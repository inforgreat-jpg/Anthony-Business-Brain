The day your business card gets taken away, who are you? I ask my clients this because most of them cannot answer it honestly. They've spent years confusing the calendar with the job. Most meetings are performance, not leadership. I watched myself do it inside my own hedge fund before I ever coached anyone. A full calendar felt like proof I mattered. The busier my inbox looked, the safer my job felt. None of it was leadership. It was theater with a dial-in link, and I built the whole set myself. Real leadership happens in the fifteen minutes before the meeting, when you decide what actually needs deciding. It happens in the one conversation you have instead of the six you scheduled. It almost never happens in the room with twelve people and one agenda item that could have been an email. Your title got you into that room. It never made you necessary in it. I see this with the senior executives I coach. They tell me some version of the same line: I've made it, so why does it still feel like something's missing. Then I look at their calendar. Back to back, every hour accounted for, none of it theirs to choose. That's the thing about blind spots. You can't see them yourself, and a full calendar is the easiest one to hide behind, because it looks exactly like work. What's one meeting you could have skipped this week and nobody would have noticed? Say it below. P.S. The executives I respect most protect empty space on their calendar like it's revenue. Because it is.
