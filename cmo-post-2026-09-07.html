There are three signs you're performing leadership instead of practicing it, and I didn't spot my own until it cost me a decision that mattered. At my last banking job, I sat in 34 meetings in one week. I could describe the color of every conference room wall. I could not name one decision I actually moved. That gap is what I now call the Presence Audit. Three questions I run with every executive before they touch their calendar again. 1. Did you say something today that changed what happened next? Not "contributed to the conversation." Changed the outcome. If no, you attended. You did not lead. 2. Could someone else have sat in your seat and nothing would be different? If yes, that meeting needed your title, not you. 3. When you left the room, did anything move: a decision, a resource, a person? Or did it just carry on without you? Presence that moves nothing is furniture with a title. None of this means doing less. Kelvin, one of the guys I mentor, cut his meeting load by 40 percent the month he bought his first laundromat business. Revenue did not drop. It grew, because he stopped mistaking attendance for output. The leaders people remember are not the ones who never missed an all-hands. They're the ones whose absence would have been noticed, because of what didn't get decided without them. Save this before your next all-hands. Run the audit on your own week first. P.S. The busiest calendar in the room has never once been proof of leadership.
