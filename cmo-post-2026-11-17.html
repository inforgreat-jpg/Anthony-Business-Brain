Full post · 1314 characters

I was 27, alone in a room with the heads of a Chinese state-owned bank, and I had no certificate, no playbook and nobody senior beside me to tell me what to say next.

I had the Penn degree and the Credit Suisse years behind me, and none of it covered this, because a room like that does not care what you studied, it cares whether you can hold your ground when the pressure is real and every instinct says to say something safe.

I paid for that room before I earned anything from it. The cost came first.

Then it kept happening. I was laid off in 2009, started a hedge fund at 30 and heard 50 no's before anyone seeded it, got laid off again in London, and later bought preschools as a numbers guy who had never run one. Every one of those rooms taught me something a course would have smoothed away.

I hold a coaching certification myself, and it gave me structure. It did not give me nerve.

I share the above not to impress anyone, rather, the key takeaway is that judgment gets built where your name is on the line. That is what the executives I coach are really looking for: someone who has been in the room, not someone who has read about it.

Anyone can choose to walk into theirs, scared and underprepared. Here's what I did. I went in.

What was the room that taught you more than any course ever did?
