Full Post

You don't jump from employee to owner. You move through three stages, and most people quit at stage one because they expect it to feel like stage three. I started as David, became Janet, then Kelvin. Here is how I read where you are. Stage 1: David Just made redundant. Panicking. The only income was a salary, and the salary stopped. I have been here twice, after Lehman in 2009 and again at a London hedge fund in 2017, so I know exactly what it does to your stomach. Readiness signal: you finally see one paycheck as a single point of failure. Stage 2: Janet Keeps the corporate job and acquires accounting practices on the side. The salary covers the bills while the practices learn to cover her. Slow, unglamorous, and it works. Readiness signal: side income that could replace the salary, and a real question about whether to fully exit. Stage 3: Kelvin Exit founder who bought laundromats and now runs a portfolio netting $500K a year. Readiness signal: you no longer need a title, a timeline or anyone's permission. I share the above not to solicit any sense of sympathy, envy or fear. Rather, the key takeaway is that feeling like David is not proof you will never be Kelvin. Nobody skips the middle. Anyone can choose to be David, Janet or Kelvin. It's a choice, and it's yours. Save this if you don't know which stage you're actually in yet. P.S. Being David twice taught me more than any title on my business card ever did.

Extend
A quick test for where you are: if your salary stopped tomorrow, how many months before you would panic? Under six, you are David. Covered and quietly buying something on the side, you are Janet. Nothing on your calendar depends on anyone's permission, you are Kelvin. The number is the honest part.
