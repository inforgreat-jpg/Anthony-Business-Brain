Being in every meeting is not leadership. It's attendance. At 27, negotiating private equity deals in Hong Kong, I sat across the table from the head of a Chinese state-owned bank. Alone. No entourage. No VP added to the invite so the calendar looked full. Just me, the terms, and what I was actually there to get. That's the version of presence that moved the deal. Not the one where you're in the room for eleven meetings a day and remembered for none of them. I coach senior executives now, and this is the pattern I see most. People who confuse being seen with being useful. They sit in on decisions that don't need them, because leaving the room feels like losing status. So they build their calendar around being seen and wonder why their actual impact keeps shrinking. Nobody remembers your meeting count. They remember what changed because you were in one. The audit I give clients isn't complicated. For every recurring meeting: does this need my judgment in the room, or does it need my decision after the fact? Most of what fills a senior calendar is the second one, dressed up as the first. Presence isn't proximity. It's the thing that's different because you showed up. What's one meeting you sat in this week that you didn't actually need to be in? P.S. The busiest calendar in the room is rarely the most powerful person in it.
