Post
The day your business card gets taken away, who are you? I have been laid off twice. Once after Lehman in 2009, once from a London hedge fund in 2017. Both times the card went first, then the inbox, then the calendar invites, and the question showed up in the mirror before anyone else asked it. Here is what I see in senior rooms now. Face time is not leadership presence. Talking is not communicating. Showing up is not showing impact. You can be first into the meeting, loudest on the call, and copied on every thread, and still have nothing left the day the card is gone. Titles give us validation. But validation that only comes from the outside leaves you empty. Your values and purpose go with you everywhere, with or without a business card. I share this not to scare you. David was just made redundant and panicking, because his salary was his only income and his title was the only answer he had to "who are you?" That is why I ask every senior client the same thing. Strip the title, the team and the meetings. What is left, and would you still choose it? Most have never been asked. Presence is not what people see you do. It is what remains when nobody is watching and nobody is paying you to be there. When was the last time you checked whether the path you're on is actually yours?

Extend

The test I give executives: write down the three things you would still say yes to if your title disappeared on Monday. Not skills on your CV. Things you would do for free. If you can't fill three lines, that is the work for this quarter, and it is better to find out now than in an exit meeting.

Humanise

I started as David, panicking about a salary that was my only income. Then I became Janet, then Kelvin. Nobody warned me the card was the easy part to lose. The hard part was admitting I had been waiting for it to tell me who I was.

CTA Amplifier

Your answer in one line below: who are you without the card? I read and reply to every comment in the first hour. No wrong answers, but a vague one tells you something too. Fall forward.
