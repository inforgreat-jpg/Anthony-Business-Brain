Most coaches were trained. They were never tested.

I say that as someone who sat through 100+ hours of ICF coach training myself,. The certificate took months. Getting laid off twice, selling almost everything I owned at 30 to start a hedge fund after 50 rejections, and then leading a team of about 50 through a preschool exit taught me what no classroom could, and I paid real money for every one of those lessons.

That is the difference I look for in anyone I let near my decisions, and it is the same one my clients are quietly checking for when they book a discovery call and do not know how to say it.

So here is the 4-question checklist I would run on any coach or mentor, me included:

  1. Have they failed at this? Not studied failure. Failed, with a name and a date attached.
  2. Have they built it? Not advised on it. Built it, with payroll and people depending on it.
  3. Did they pay for the mistake? Their own money, their own reputation, their own sleep.
  4. Can they say "if I were in your position" and mean it? Because they have actually stood there.

If the answer to all four is no, you are paying for a playbook, not a mentor.

I share this not to run down my own profession, rather, the key takeaway is that a senior professional deserves to know who is sitting across the table before they hand over a cent.

P.S. Screenshot this before your next discovery call with any coach, then repost it for the friend who is about to sign.

📌 Extend

Add a fifth question if you want to go deeper: ask them to describe the worst quarter they ever had and what it cost them personally. People who were tested answer in specifics, with numbers and names. People who were only trained answer in principles. You can hear the difference in about thirty seconds.

📌 Humanise

I will be honest about why I wrote this. At 30 I was jobless, I had sold almost everything I owned, and I collected 50 rejections before anyone seeded my hedge fund. Nobody in a classroom could have told me what that felt like at 2am. The people who helped most were the ones who had been through their own version of it.
